# NOTICE — NIST SP 800-53 Revision 5

**Source.** *Security and Privacy Controls for Information Systems and Organizations*,
NIST Special Publication 800-53 Revision 5, National Institute of Standards and
Technology, U.S. Department of Commerce, as published by NIST in machine-readable form:
the OSCAL catalog `NIST_SP-800-53_rev5_catalog.json` from the `usnistgov/oscal-content`
repository, titled *Electronic (OSCAL) Version of NIST SP 800-53 Rev 5.2.0 Controls and SP
800-53A Rev 5.2.0 Assessment Procedures*, version 5.2.0, last modified 2026-10-22.
<https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.SP.800-53r5>

**Licence / status.** A work of the United States federal government. Under 17 U.S.C.
§105 it is **not subject to copyright protection in the United States** and is in the
public domain. NIST asks that it be cited, which is what this file is for. NIST does not
endorse this repository or any product; "NIST" is used here only to identify the source of
the taxonomy.

**What is transcribed here.** Structure only: the twenty control families as `domain`
nodes and their 300 base controls as `control` nodes, each with OSCAL's identifier
(`AC-2`) and title ("Account Management"). The 24 controls the catalog marks withdrawn are
left out and counted in `model.yaml`'s header. Control enhancements, statements,
parameters, discussion and the SP 800-53B baselines are **not** included: the catalog is
the authority on what a control requires, and a paraphrase sitting in a YAML file gets
quoted back as if it were the standard. An organisation that maps at the enhancement
grain can regenerate the pack with enhancements and re-pin it.

**How it was produced.** Not transcribed: generated by `tools/oscal_to_pack.py` in this
repository from the catalog file named above (SHA-256 `64ca6b41…115eeb`), which is NIST's
own machine-readable publication of the controls. Re-running the script against a later
catalog is the whole upgrade procedure; the diff of `model.yaml` is then the list of what
NIST changed.

**Verification status: structure not yet verified.** Two things are true at once here,
and collapsing them into one word is exactly what this section exists to prevent.

*What has been done (2026-10-25).* Every node was generated from the primary source by
code, so identifiers, titles and parentage are exactly what the catalog says, and the
counts (20 families, 300 base controls, 24 withdrawn) were read off the generator's output.

*What has not been done.* No person has read the publication against this pack. This
repository defines a verified pack as one **a human** has read the primary source for and
walked the taxonomy against — and generation from OSCAL, however exact, confirms only that
the pack says what the catalog file says, not that the selection (base controls, no
enhancements, withdrawn ones dropped) is the grain a reader will take it to be. Until a
person does that reading and replaces this section with a dated statement of it, treat the
pack as a draft yardstick: do not cite these nodes as evidence of what the source
requires, and do not let one of their gaps alone drive a decision.

**What this pack is for.** A yardstick for `python -m easkills align`: a catalogue of
control areas an architecture's requirements, constraints and principles are measured
against, so an unaddressed control is a named gap (`ALN004`) and an element rated high
that no control anchors is named too (`ALN009`). Coverage of a control by a model element
is **not** a statement of implementation, assessment or authorization — those are
judgements made with evidence this tooling does not hold.

**Integrity.** `model.yaml` and this file are pinned in `SHA256SUMS`. `align` refuses to
read the pack if either byte-changes (`ALN001`); re-pin with
`python -m easkills pin-reference --dir references/nist-sp-800-53-r5` only for a
deliberate, reviewed update — a regenerated catalog, or the human reading above.
